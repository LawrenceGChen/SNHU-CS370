What do computer scientists do and why does it matter?

Computer scientists systematically study computation, information processing, and problem-solving. They are the designers of the algorithms, architectures, and systems that allow us to solve complex problems, manipulate data, and interact with technology. They do this by designing algorithms and theory, building systems and software, advancing artificial intelligence and machine learning, ensuring cybersecurity and cryptography, and managing and analyzing big data. This work matters because it drives global progress, empowers every industry, connects the world, and automates and optimizes repetitive and dangerous tasks.

How do I approach a problem as a computer scientist?

As a computer scientist, problems can be approached from multiple angles. Depending on the problem at hand, I may approach a problem top-down or bottom-up. Top-down allows me to design the overall architecture of the solution for the problem before working out the details and minutiae of the program, whereas bottom-up allows me to start with the raw data and process it one step at a time before reaching the final result.

What are my ethical responsibilities to the end user and the organization?

My ethical responsibilities are outlined by the code of ethics posted by the IEEE Computer Society.
